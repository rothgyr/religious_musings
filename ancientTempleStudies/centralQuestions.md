## Record Keeping Roles In Israel
In Nephi's account (Book of Mormon 1 Nephi) of Laban, he references the Elders of the Jews, notes that Laban has authority, access to metal codices, servants, etc. According to monarchic-Judah's tradition, in which roles would Laban's description likely land?

### Major Factions in Nephi's Time

1. (Pro Jeremiah) The scribal/palace party — Shaphan's house. Shaphan the sofer under Josiah; sons Ahikam, Elasah, Gemariah; grandson Gedaliah. They alone side with Jeremiah: Ahikam saves him from the mob (Jer 26:24), Gemariah begs Jehoiakim not to burn the scroll and lends his temple chamber for Baruch's reading, Elasah carries his letter to Babylon, and Gedaliah shelters him at Mizpah. As royal secretaries they had direct access to the palace archives and temple records — Shaphan handled the temple funds and read the discovered Torah. They were the pro-Babylonian diplomacy faction and enjoyed the king's ear even when he ignored them. Their bullae (Shaphan, Gemariah) survive.

2. (Josiah's Promoted, probably Jeremiah's enemies) The Zadokite temple hierarchy. Hilkiah → Shallum → Azariah → Seraiah (executed at Riblah, 586), plus Zephaniah the mishneh, and the Pashhur and Immer houses. Josiah's centralization dispossessed the rural priests (hence Jeremiah of Anathoth as outsider). Access to the Temple, its treasury, and its liturgical records; they prosecuted Jeremiah as a false prophet and blasphemer.

3. (Jeremiah's enemies) The war-party nobility/military. Jehucal son of Shelemiah, Gedaliah son of Pashhur, Shephatiah, Pashhur son of Malchiah — named together in Jer 38:1 demanding Jeremiah's death; their bullae were excavated meters apart in the City of David.

The Elders (zeqenim) sat across all three — town gates and national council — but gravitated toward the Shaphanide peace line, since they bore the cost of siege and deportation.


### Notes on each faction
Who was "promoted" after Josiah’s demotions?
You are correct: Josiah’s reform (2 Kgs 22–23) was a Zadokite coup that demoted the rural, Abiatharid-line priests (Jeremiah’s class) and elevated the Jerusalem-based, urban Zadokites.

The "Promoted" Class:

The Urban Zadokites: Hilkiah and his house. They controlled the Temple Treasury (2 Kgs 22:4–7) and the Temple Administration. Josiah gave them the mandate to purge the countryside.
The Sagan (Deputy High Priest): The "priests of the second rank" (2 Kgs 23:4) were the bureaucratic arm of the high priest. They managed the day-to-day logistics, the records, and the enforcement of the reform.
The Temple Gatekeepers/Keepers of the Threshold: These were the security and custodial staff of the Temple. They were given authority to seize and burn idolatrous objects. They were the "priestly police."
The Demoted Class:

The Kohanim of the High Places (Bamot): The country priests of Judah’s towns (Jer 1:1, 48–53; 2 Kgs 23:8–9). They were stripped of the right to serve at Jerusalem’s altar (though allowed to eat matzah like their brothers). They lost their political voice, their income from the central sanctuary, and their social status. Jeremiah himself was one of these — a priest from Anathoth (a rural town) who had no standing in the Temple hierarchy.

### General Notes on the Situation
The Key Conflict: Jeremiah was at odds with Hananiah because Hananiah’s message (victory in 2 years) was the direct opposite of Jeremiah’s (surrender or die in 7 years). Hananiah had the king’s ear and the people’s support. Jeremiah had the Shaphanide scribal faction’s protection and, ultimately, the Babylonian victory’s vindication.

The Irony: The Zadokite high priesthood (Hilkiah’s line) initially supported Josiah’s reform (which demoted Jeremiah’s class), but later persecuted Jeremiah (Hilkiah’s successor Seraiah) because Jeremiah’s theology threatened the Temple’s existence. The scribal family (Shaphan) protected Jeremiah because his theology (covenant accountability) validated the Torah scroll their own house had helped discover.




## Ancient Egyption
Recognizing that Egypt has several epochs spanning thousands of years - how consistent is all Egyptian writing/cryptography/record keeping with that of the rosetta stone? What kind of text characters, styles, etc. does the Rosetta Stone have? Does it cover all egyptian?

A:
Stage	Understanding	Key reason	Main challenges
Old Egyptian	Very well	Clear texts, simple grammar	Fragmentary inscriptions, a few ambiguous readings
Middle Egyptian	Extremely well	Huge literary corpus, centuries of study	Minor unresolved passages
Late Egyptian	Well	Massive papyrus archive	Colloquial/formal register confusion
Demotic	Well (but niche)	Deciphered alongside hieroglyphs	Fewer specialists, abstract script
Coptic	Fully understood	Living tradition, alphabetic, vocalized	Dialectal variation, Greek loanwords
