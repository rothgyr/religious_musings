# What Does it Mean to be a Priest?

## Oaths and Orders

It's a cold december night in Denver, CO. I'm traveling with friends through the west to
see Utah, and the rockies during the winter, and I'm on my way back home to Kansas City.
I'm on my knees, praying for forgiveness for sins, knowing that if I talk to my bishop,
who has mission papers waiting at home, he might put them away for another six months.

I'm getting ready for a mission. For other guys, that might mean working to save up eleven
thousand dollars, or finishing up a semester or two before they turn 19. For me, it's
leaving behind bad habits and working with a bishop who's rigid in my preparations. I've
been working with him for a year and a half, and he's held me to a high standard.

Most of my friends have been gone on missions for that whole time while I linger. And
linger. And linger.

My family must be judging me. Must be wondering if I'm just a dead beat. My mom's been
gone for three years. My relationship with my dad is strained. Home feels like black hole.

And here I am on my knees, wondering - should I talk to the bishop? Can I hide my recent
relapse and just go as I am?

Or will I yield my will to God's, and go "worthily"?

I'm in my uncle's house in Denver, in a room by myself, praying my heart out, terrified of
what another six months will look like, full of answering people's questions of when I
will go with a simple "I'm still getting ready".

I'm in despair.

In the end, I commit to God that I will speak with my bishop.

My life boasts few moments of "visions", or big "revelations", but I think what happens
next counts. I don't see a spiritual tv screen pop up with a video. There are no sounds.

It's like any other memory that pops up in your mind. Instantaneous. Powerful.

I see myself kneeling at a throne, swearing an Oath. An Oath to serve my King. A man
who--though all powerful--will only spend a short 3 years showing that power. The span of
humanity is thousands of years long, and yet he will only be present for a short stint. I
am swearing an Oath like others have, that I will do what he would do if he were present.

The vision is brief, but life changing. I speak with my bishop when I get home. We send my
application papers the same week. Within six months, I am on a mission in Brazil,
accumulating incredible experiences I might never have seen if I had simply gone "as I
was".

This is my first experience with Oaths.

## Knights, Chivalric Orders, Kings, and Priesthoods

I've always had a fascination with knights, chivalry, castles, medieval history, etc.

As a nerdy homeschooled kid, I spent whole history units on Gothic Cathedrals and
architecture. I built lego castles for fun. We studied the _Magna Carta_, the Reformation
and Protestantism vs Catholicism at length. The crusades, etc.

Some of the books we read were campy. Some were blatant conservative propaganda. Some were
downright fascinating.

One book was about Stephen Langton, author of the _Magna Carter_. The book was intended to
talk about the roots of the US Constitution, but what I remember most was how it opened by
talking about [fealty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fealty) of a vassal to the king, and
gaining [fiefdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) after swearing fealty to a king.
Notably, it had pictures,
demonstrating "[homage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_(feudal))" or the pledge of
fealty.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e5/Hommage_au_Moyen_Age_-_miniature.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)

In return for homage or the pledge of fealty, the king grants to vassal fiefdom, or parts
of his kingdom to care for and essentially own. It was a cool book and taught about how
King John was put in his place by his peers (the other dukes) and vassals and Parliament
was formed as a result.

Obviously it left a lasting impression.

Another section in our studies that fascinated me was the crusades, and knightly or
priestly orders. It was common for knights to enter oaths to protect the pilgrims
traveling East to visit Jerusalem. Different orders of knights formed. The Teutonic order
went on a mission to lithuania and formed their own country which ended
up [transforming into Prussia/Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order#16th_century_and_aftermath)
someday. It's a cool story, and I still love learning more about it to this day. In old
times, before they annointed a king, the Hochmeister (Grandmaster) of their order was the
ruler of the kingdom.

Different [religious orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_order_(Catholic))
rose in the Catholic church as well.

I think the 90s were hard on youth in the church, at least where I was growing up in
Kansas City. A lot of my older siblings got into drugs, bad relationships or worse as
teens. People going on missions happened, but it wasn't super common.

Around the time I was about eleven, our ward called a new Young Men's president. Brock
Guernsey was _deep_ into the boy scouting tradition, and it turns out, _deep_ into history
and temple doctrines. During his tenure, almost every teen in the youth program got their
Eagle Scout status, and the vast majority of us served missions.

At sixteen, I was in the priests quorum and he spent many lessons on temple preparation.
He would bring in books and show us ancient carvings of the original apostles with marks
on their clothes. He delved into the scriptures with us and showed us examples of temple
instructions in Genesis and the Pearl of Great Price, and even some books in the
Apocrypha.

He told us we would be going to the temple and receiving a new name, annointings,
investiture, etc., in the same way that kings are [annointed behind a veil](https://en.
wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_the_British_monarch#Anointing), given
[regnal names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regnal_name), and so much more.

But I don't think it was until April 2008 that I really started to see my priesthood
ordination and the temple ordinances as being connected. I had heard people talk about
the "Oath and Covenant of the Priesthood" as a young man, but it never seemed to click for
me. In April 2008, Henry Eyering gave a talk entitled
_[Faith and the Oath and Covenant of the Priesthood](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.
org/study/general-conference/2008/04/faith-and-the-oath-and-covenant-of-the-priesthood?
lang=eng)_ and it made me ask "when did God make an Oath to me? When was a covenant made?"
As far as I could tell, I had simply talked to my leaders and been ordained to the
Melchizedek Priesthood, and that was that.

While reading the Book of Mormon one day, I happened upon Alma 12-13 and realized it was
specifically talking about the temple ceremony - that God sent angels to teach Adam and
Even, and then

> ordained ordained [them into the Holy Priesthood, after the Order of the Son of God]
> , to teach these things unto the people. [...] Now they were ordained after this
> manner—being called with a holy calling, and ordained with a holy ordinance, and
> taking upon them the high priesthood of the holy order, which calling, and ordinance,
> and high priesthood, is without beginning or end— 9 Thus they become high priests
> forever, after the order of the Son, the Only Begotten of the Father, who is
> without beginning of days or end of years, who is full of grace, equity, and truth.
> And thus it is. Amen.

"Ordained with a holy ordinance". I realized that the conferral of the Melchizidek
Priesthood would ideally happen at the same time that as the initiatory ordinance and
endowment are given. In fact, when officiating for the dead, the conferral of priesthood
office comprises the opening lines of the initiatory ordinance. As a result, I rarely use
terms like "the initiatory" or "have the priesthood", but prefer to look at them as an
initiation into the priesthood.

A whole other work of writing could cover the roles of priests, the High Priest of Israel,
what it means to be Kings and Queens, Priests and Priestesses, or of Moses' intentions of
Israel being a kingdom of priests who communed personally with God but the nation backed
away from it and ended up with only one high priest.

I want to get back on track and say that there are multiple Orders of Priesthood, and the
highest, which requires Oaths, Covenants, Annointings, Washings, Regnal Names, etc. is
Melchizedek, or Holy Priesthood, after the Order of the Son of God.

## Kingdoms, Principalities, Powers

We've been at this for days. My sister Aurelia is too smart let simple answers go
unchecked. She asks questions in a way that exposes holes in my knowledge left and right.
Things I've assumed were true are called into question. I find myself looking for new
sources. I find myself understanding that not all my old and reliable sources have been
correct.

"Why does my [evangelical] husband need to be baptized in our church? He's a better
christian than any of us. It's _obvious_ he has a great relationship with Jesus."

My answers of priesthood authority land on skeptical ears. I try my best to defend against
accusations of gate keeping, people wanting power, about authority over others, etc. and
I'm met with arguments that Jesus saves, not church or priesthood.

Is she even wrong, though?

Two years transpire. A global pandemic has me serving bread and water in a messy house to
rowdy children each sunday. Questions about cultural practices plague my mind, and I find
myself redefining and taking a different ownership over tithing, fasting, service, even
the way I pray. I'm trying to be a more sincere friend to God.

And now, I'm back home in Kansas City for a high school orchestra reunion, and Aurelia's
here with me, sitting at my brother's kitchen table, debating agency, the state of souls,
and forfeiture of "kingdoms of glory". Again, she's got big questions, big experiences,
and suddenly my answers are starting to seem poorly thought out, trite, or even downright
condescending.

Within a week, I'm giving a talk in church, and studying, when I find Joseph Smith's King
Follett discourse
([part 1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1971/04/the-king-follett-sermon?lang=eng),
[part 2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1971/05/the-king-follett-sermon?lang=eng))
and find some inconsistencies with what I've been taught, and the question lingers - what
is the "Celestial" kingdom?

I voraciously pour over the scriptures and other sources, looking for answers, and to my
surprise, I find that celestial, terrestrial, and telestial references are actually about
resurrections, not places where we end up. This likewise triggers years of study and
interesting findings, but those aren't in the scope of this story.

I see something new. Where my whole life has been spent focusing on "personal salvation",
I've had blinders on against how often Jesus and Joseph Smith talked about the "kingdom of
heaven", literally
a "[celestial](https://webstersdictionary1828.com/Dictionary/celestial)"
kingdom. In their references to such a kingdom, I rarely see "personal worthiness"
mentioned, but rather, doing the will of the king, and following instructions as a group.

Where we are accused of "gatekeeping", I'm realizing that priesthood is about being a
minister, an officer in the kingdom. Not to be authoritarians, but to do the King's
bidding, protecting the citizens, and making sure that needs are met in accordance with
the king's will.

It is much
to [Jesus' own point](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/mark/10?lang=eng&id=p42-p45#p42):

> 42 But Jesus [...] saith unto them, Ye know that they which are accounted to rule over
the Gentiles exercise lordship over them; and their great ones exercise authority upon
them.
>
> 43 But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will be great among you, shall be
> your minister:
>
> 44 And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all.
>
> 45 For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give
his life a ransom for many.

In my search for answers, I happen upon
a [vision that Lorenzo Dow Young (Brigham Young's brother) had](https://www.latterdaysainthaven.com/vision.htm),
where he saw a heavenly city, divided into two parts, with God's mansion in the center. A
telestial outer city, a terrestrial inner city, and God's
"Celestial" mansion or court.

I watch Pride and Prejudice and realize that when Sir William Lucas offers to introduce
someone at the Court of St James, he's inferring that as a Knight, he can introduce people
to the king. Just because you're a citizen of the kingdom of heaven by being saved, does
not mean you are entitled to mingle at the king's court, or run his errands.

I begin to believe that Priesthood, recieved in exchange for homage and fealty, earns you
fiefdoms in the kingdom, where you are to carry out the king's judgement, laws, benefits
to the citizens, etc.

To represent the king to those cannot be with personally.

## The King's Work: My Oath, My Covenant

It's 2001. I'm turning fifteen soon. My brother Ben came home from his mission last year,
and something came back with him. He's different than he was when I was a kid. He's
inspiring. He's dedicated to God. To Truth.

My whole family has changed. We listen to hymns in the car. We painted the house. I'm
reading the book of mormon again. There's an electricity in the air.

I feel the Holy Ghost in my heart. A lot, actually.

I'm sitting by the sliding back door at home on the horribly stained green carpet in the
dining room, basking in the heat of the February sun as it shines through the huge glass
doors. I'm reading Les Miserables, just like my grandparents did nearly fifty years ago
when my grandma was pregnant with my Mom, Cosette. The book is a roller coaster.

It's also really boring sometimes.

But Victor Hugo's ramblings and musings in this chapter catch my attention. I've been
wanting to serve God more. To give back. To act on all the feelings I've been having.
Apparently Hugo spent some time to write about his thoughts on the same subject. "What's
the point of a convent or abbey? People go there to... be holy? Nothing gets done. No good
is accomplished..."

Yeah Hugo, you're totally right.

I close my eyes and say a prayer. "I want to do something. But what should I _do_? Read
more scriptures? Go to more church?" These seem like lame answers.

It's never a voice in my head, but I've since learned to recognize when words put
themselves incredibly clearly in my mind.

_When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God._

## I will be on your right hand and on your left

I'm driving my car with the windows cracked even though it's freezing outside. I'm on my
way home from the gym in Orem, making the 30 minute drive home in 7:15 AM traffic, and I
don't want the windows fogging up with all the condensation from overheated body. Since
the wind is noisy, I've got some trashy music playing at high volume. Exactly everything
they told me "offends the spirit."

OK, maybe not _everything_, but I think you get the point.

It's been sixteen years since that night in Denver when I had a... vision? Recollection?
Revelation? I served a mission and as time goes by, I realize that many of the
_remarkable_ experiences I had were kind of peculiar to my mission. Did we speak in
tongues? I'm pretty sure I did. Did we heal the sick? Yes. Did we cast out devils in the
name of Jesus? Yes. Did I successfully invoke the ministry of angels? Yes.

Would any of those thing have happened if I had gone "as I was"?

No.

But I don't necessarily believe that those things happened because I was special. Or
because I was even particularly "worthy".

It's because I was on God's errand. And when I am on his errand, as he says:

> And as I said unto mine apostles, even so I say unto you, for you are mine apostles,
even God’s high priests [...] ye are my friends [...]
>
> And whoso receiveth you, there I will be also, for I will go before your face. I
> will be on your right hand and on your left, and my Spirit shall be in your hearts, and
mine angels round about you, to bear you up.

I'm driving my car, thinking about life. Experiences. Things I've done. Some things I'm
not proud of. Some things that were kind. Some things that have taken sacrifice.

_You are my friend_.

It hits different than what we expect in songs like _How Great Thou Art_, or _When He
Comes Again_, where you expect some formal audience to declare things to the Jesus you
finally get to meet.

It hits different than the times I've been forgiven.

I've felt Jesus' love for me in _spite_ of what I do so many times, that I baffles me to
be loved _because_ of what I do.

And to be sure, I still have more of the "I love you in spite of that thing" than I have
of the latter -- but it sticks with me that while listening to a song with a bunch of F
bombs and crazy beats, I felt that briefest visit, to tell me "thank you".

Jesus may have undergone a huge Passion to save me, but the work of saving me has looked
more like sending people to teach me. Sending the Holy Ghost to confirm it. Keeping me
strong through others' examples at church, and in life in general. The biggest differences
aren't made in great speeches, or huge undertakings.

It's in the sunday school teacher taking a few minutes to say that something's been on
their mind a lot, and they need to talk about it, or someone sending a text when I'm
feeling overwhelmed and telling me I'm doing a great job. Or my brother leaving a
voicemail to share his testimony at midnight, because he can't just experience something
big alone.

It's in things like paying fast offerings, playing piano for church meetings, and maybe
making a meaningful comment at church, or even just showing up that are acts of salvation.
Fulfilling oaths.
