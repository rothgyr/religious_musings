# What Does it Mean to be a Priest?

## Oaths and Orders

It's a cold december night in Denver, CO. I'm traveling with friends through the west to see Utah, and the rockies
during the winter, and I'm on my way back home to Kansas City. I'm on my knees, praying for forgiveness for sins,
knowing that if I talk to my bishop, who has mission papers waiting at home, he might put them away for another six
months.

I'm getting ready for a mission. For other guys, that might mean working to save up eleven thousand dollars, or
finishing up a semester or two before they turn 19. For me, it's leaving behind bad habits and working with a bishop
who's rigid in my preparations. I've been working with him for a year and a half, and he's held me to a high standard.

Most of my friends have been gone on missions for that whole time while I linger. And linger. And linger.

My family must be judging me. Must be wondering if I'm just a dead beat. My mom's been gone for three years. My
relationship with my dad is strained. Home feels like black hole.

And here I am on my knees, wondering - should I talk to the bishop? Can I hide my recent relapse and just go as I am?

Or will I yield my will to God's, and go "worthily"?

I'm in my uncle's house in Denver, in a room by myself, praying my heart out, terrified of what another six months will
look like,
full of answering people's questions of when I will go with a simple "I'm still getting ready".

I'm in despair.

In the end, I commit to God that I will speak with my bishop.

My life boasts few moments of "visions", or big "revelations", but I think what happens next counts. I don't see a
spiritual tv screen pop up with a video. There are no sounds.

It's like any other memory that pops up in your mind. Instantaneous. Powerful.

I see myself kneeling at a throne, swearing an Oath. An Oath to serve my King. A man who--though all powerful--will only
spend a short 3 years showing that power. The span of humanity is thousands of years long, and yet he will only be
present for a short stint. I am swearing an Oath like others have, that I will do what he would do if he were present.

The vision is brief, but life changing. I speak with my bishop when I get home. We send my application papers the same
week. Within six months, I am on a mission in Brazil, accumulating incredible experiences I might never have seen if I
had simply gone "as I was".

This is my first experience with Oaths.

## Knights, Chivalric Orders, Kings, and Priesthoods

I've always had a fascination with knights, chivalry, castles, medieval history, etc.

As a nerdy homeschooled kid, I spent whole history units on Gothic Cathedrals and architecture. I built lego castles for fun. We studied the _Magna Carta_, the Reformation and Protestantism vs Catholicism at length. The crusades, etc.

Some of the books we read were campy. Some were blatant conservative propaganda. Some were downright fascinating.

One book was about Stephen Langton, author of the _Magna Carter_. The book was intended to talk about the roots of the US Constitution, but what I remember most was how it opened by talking about [fealty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fealty) of a vassal to the king, and gaining [fiefdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) after swearing fealty to a king. Notably, it had pictures, demonstrating "[homage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_(feudal))" or the pledge of fealty. 

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e5/Hommage_au_Moyen_Age_-_miniature.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)

In return for homage or the pledge of fealty, the king grants to vassal fiefdom, or parts of his kingdom to care for and essentially own. It was a cool book and taught about how King John was put in his place by his peers (the other dukes) and vassals and Parliament was formed as a result.

Obviously it left a lasting impression.

Another section in our studies that fascinated me was the crusades, and knightly or priestly orders. It was common for knights to enter oaths to protect the pilgrims traveling East to visit Jerusalem. Different orders of knights formed. The Teutonic order went on a mission to lithuania and formed their own country which ended up [transforming into Prussia/Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order#16th_century_and_aftermath) someday. It's a cool story, and I still love learning more about it to this day. In old times, before they annointed a king, the Hochmeister (Grandmaster) of their order was the ruler of the kingdom.

Different [religious orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_order_(Catholic)) rose in the Catholic church as well.

that exposes holes in my knowledge left and right. Things I've assumed were true as called into question. I find myself
looking for sources. I find myself understanding that not all my sources have been correct.

"Why does my husband need to be baptized in our church? He's a better christian than any of us."

My answers of priesthood authority land on skeptical ears. I try my best to defend against accusations of gate keeping,
people wanting power, about authority over others, etc. and am met with arguments that Jesus saves, not church or
priesthood.

Is she wrong, though?

Two years transpire. A global pandemic has me serving bread and water in a messy house to rowdy children each sunday.
Questions about culutral practices plague my mind, and I find myself redefining and taking a different ownership over
tithing, fasting, service, even the way I pray. I'm trying to be a more sincere friend to God.

And now, I'm back home in Kansas City for a high school orchestra reunion, and Aurelia's here with me, sitting at my
brother's kitchen table, debating agency, the state of souls, and forfeiture of "kingdoms of glory". Again, she's got
big questions, big experiences, and suddenly my answers are starting to seem poorly thought out, trite, or even
downright condescending.

Within a week, I'm giving a talk in church, and studying, when I find Joseph Smith's King Follett discourse and find
some inconsistencies with what I've been taught, and the question lingers - what is the "Celestial" kingdom?

celestial, terrestrial, and telestial references are actually about resurrections, not places where we end up. I've been

instructions as a group.

King's bidding, protecting the citizens, and making sure that needs are met in accordance with the king's will.
